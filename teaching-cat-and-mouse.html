The Cat and the Mouse Method

A simple way to quiet a mind that's running too many programs at once — and start choosing which thoughts actually deserve your attention.

Mind Tool 01 · ~3 min watch · Trunk: Awareness
Chapters

Overview

One thought pulls you in, and suddenly you're not here anymore.

You're doing fine, and then something pops up — a loop, a worry, a conversation you're re-running for the fifth time — and without noticing it, you've left the present moment entirely.

Kurt compares this to a computer running too many programs in the background: the more open tabs your mind is holding, the less capacity you have for what's actually in front of you. The Cat and the Mouse Method is a simple, repeatable way to notice each thought as it appears and choose, one at a time, whether it deserves your energy.

Main Ideas

What this teaching is actually saying.

01

Too many tabs open

Like a computer running 10 or 20 background programs, a mind full of unattended thoughts has far less capacity left for the task — or person — in front of you.

02

You're the cat, thoughts are the mice

Picture your thoughts as mice appearing one at a time from a hole. You're the cat — you simply observe each one as it appears.

03

Not every thought needs your energy

Some thoughts can be let go immediately. Others are worth capturing to unpack later. The practice is choosing, moment to moment, which is which.

04

It's cumulative

Every thought you release closes another tab. Do this consistently and the background noise thins out — clarity compounds over time.

05

Single point focus

The state that emerges once enough noise clears: your attention fully available to whatever is actually in front of you.

06

Kaizen — one thought at a time

This isn't about controlling the mind all at once. It's continuous improvement: small releases, repeated, that compound.

0:00Have you ever noticed how one thought can take over your entire mind? You're doing totally fine and then one thing pops up and suddenly you're caught in a loop replaying something, trying to figure something out or going over something that actually doesn't need your attention. And before you know it, you're not really here anymore. You're lost somewhere in your own mind.

0:33What's happening is you've got too many things running at the same time. Let's think about this like a computer. If you have 10 or 20 programs open all running in the background, what happens? It greatly reduces the capacity you actually have left for the task in front of you. The same thing happens with your mind. The more thoughts running in the background, the less presence you actually have. And with less presence comes less clarity, less focus, and less connection to what's happening right now.

1:05So, here's a simple way to start working with that. I call it the cat and the mouse method. Think of your thoughts like mice coming out of a hole one at a time. And you, you're the cat. Each time a thought appears, you observe it. And if it's not something you need to be focused on, working through, or intentionally giving your attention to, you let it go.

1:38Not every thought needs your energy. Not every thought needs to be solved. Of course, some thoughts you may want to keep and unpack. Some you may want to explore later. You can capture them, write them down, or come back to them. But in the moment, you're choosing which stays and what goes.

2:12This process is also cumulative. Every time you do it, you reduce some of the noise. You close another tab. And over time, your mind starts to become clear. You can begin to experience this beautiful state of being which I call single point focus where your attention is fully available to what's in front of you. And then what happens? Your clarity increases. Your decisions improve. You start operating from a deeper level of awareness.

2:38This doesn't happen all at once. It happens one thought at a time. There's a Japanese concept called kaizen, which means continuous improvement. Small changes over time that compound. And that's what this is, too. You're not trying to control your mind. You're learning how to work with it, one thought at a time.

Sit With This

Practice It Before You Move On

There are no correct answers. Write what genuinely feels true.

  1. 01

    What thought took over your mind today — and did it actually need your attention?

  2. 02

    Which thoughts are mice you can simply let pass? Which ones are worth catching and unpacking later?

  3. 03

    How many "tabs" do you tend to leave open in the background — and what would closing even one change today?

Try This

For the next ten minutes, notice each thought as it appears. Ask: does this need me right now? If not, let it pass. If yes, capture it in a few words and return to it later. One thought at a time.
